## Patterns by segment

### MRO / maintenance facilities
- **Duncan Aviation** — IG ~13K, LinkedIn ~34K. Leads with scale + heritage ("world's largest
  privately owned business jet service provider", 70-year anniversary). LinkedIn mixes market
  commentary (pre-owned market), capability news and heavy recruiting. Marketing fully in-house.
  - Adopt: heritage/milestone framing, LinkedIn as the main B2B channel, recruiting as a steady pillar.
  - Avoid for 305 SKY: competing on size — compete on attention, transparency and speed instead.
- **Banyan Air Service (FXE, Fort Lauderdale)** — local benchmark. FBO + MRO, factory-authorized
  for Beechcraft, Piaggio, Quest, HondaJet; FAA + EASA repair station; publicized extended shifts
  to cut turnaround. Press-release driven presence; social presence not confirmed in research.
  - Lesson: turnaround/hours and OEM authorizations are the claims local buyers compare.
- General MRO observations: LinkedIn dominates for B2B decision makers (DOMs, procurement); Instagram
  works for behind-the-scenes, culture and hiring; buyers search technical keywords (types, certificates).

### Management / charter (borrow the elite look)
- **VistaJet** — IG ~166K. Signature, consistent livery (silver/red) as the visual anchor; content as
  lifestyle curator (destinations, cabin service, crew); runs travel/lifestyle editorial on its site
  ("Stories") and pushes it through social; POV cabin videos gain traction on short-form video.
- **Flexjet** — IG ~102K. Bio leads with heritage (30 years), safety and the "Red Label" experience —
  a named premium standard used as a brand device.
  - Adopt: a named standard for 305 SKY's workmanship (e.g. "the 305 Standard" [CONFIRM]) repeated
    across posts; a tight colour signature (black/gold) as recognisable as VistaJet's red.
- **Solairus / Clay Lacy** — Solairus closed the acquisition of Clay Lacy's management & charter
  divisions (~Oct 1 2026); Clay Lacy refocuses on FBO, maintenance and real estate. Management
  companies are a key B2B audience: they choose shops for dozens of aircraft. LinkedIn content that
  speaks to managers (reporting, communication, documentation) is high-value.
- Category consensus: quality over quantity; avoid overt promotion; behind-the-scenes operational
  excellence, crew introductions, client stories with privacy protections.

### Aircraft sales / brokerage
- **Guardian Jet** — IG ~14K (only ~385 posts — proof that a low cadence of very polished posts works);
  LinkedIn ~2.4K. Uses rankings ("#1 in pre-owned Bombardier sales" lists), team promotions, trade-press coverage.
- **Jetcraft** — LinkedIn ~8.7K. Global network message; events/panels; blog + monthly newsletter
  articles redistributed on social to build authority.
  - Adopt: earned third-party proof (press mentions, rankings, awards) reshared; newsletter-to-social pipeline.
  - Opportunity: brokers need pre-buy inspections — LinkedIn content on thorough, neutral pre-buys targets them.

## Takeaways for 305 SKY
1. Nobody in South Florida MRO owns "transparent, premium, personal" on social — that's the gap.
2. Charter/sales brands win on visual consistency; MROs win on proof and people. Combine both.
3. LinkedIn is where contracts come from; Instagram is where the brand becomes desirable and
   recruits; Facebook is where owners and the local community recognise you.
4. Low volume is fine if every post is excellent and recurring series make the feed recognizable.
