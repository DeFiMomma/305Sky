## How the map pack is decided

Google ranks local results on three things: **relevance** (does the profile and its website match
the search), **distance** (how close the profile is to the searcher or the town in the search) and
**prominence** (reviews, links, mentions, how well known the business is). Distance can't be
changed, so the work goes into relevance and prominence.

Expert-survey weights for the local pack (Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors):
GBP signals 32%, reviews 20%, on-page (website) 15%, behavioral 9%, links 8%, citations 6%,
personalization 6%, social 4%. These are expert opinion, not Google's formula, but they set
priorities: **profile → reviews → website pages → links/citations.**

The strongest levers, in order:

1. **Primary GBP category.** The single biggest relevance factor. Use the closest true category
   (e.g. "Aircraft maintenance company") and add every honest secondary category Google offers
   (check the dropdown; candidates include avionics, aircraft dealer, aviation consultant).
2. **Reviews:** total count, how recent they are, a steady pace, star rating, and **review text
   that names the service, the aircraft and the airport** ("Citation 560XL phase inspection at
   FLL"). Answer every review, and use the service words naturally in the reply.
3. **Landing page the profile links to.** Each profile links to its own location page
   (`/fort-lauderdale-fll-aircraft-maintenance`, not the home page) whose title, H1 and content
   name the city, airport code and services, with matching name/address/phone (NAP) and
   LocalBusiness schema.
4. **Aircraft-type and service pages** (Challenger, Citation, Learjet, King Air, Gulfstream,
   Falcon, Global, AOG) linked from every location page. Most of the aircraft-type keywords are
   searched by operators nationwide and often show normal (organic) results more than a map pack,
   so these pages win both.
5. **Prominence:** links and mentions from aviation sites (airport tenant directories, AirNav,
   FlightAware, GlobalAir, AEA, NBAA, AOPA, Aviation Week marketplace, OEM/vendor dealer lists,
   local chambers, aviation press), plus consistent NAP everywhere.
6. **Activity:** photos (geo-relevant, real jobs, techs, hangar, signage), weekly posts, products/
   services filled in with descriptions, accurate hours (AOG 24/7 only if a human truly answers).

## Hard rules (breaking them gets profiles suspended or filtered)

- **Business name = real-world name.** "305 SKY", not "305 SKY Challenger Maintenance FLL".
  Keyword-stuffed names are the most common reason for suspension. Never suggest it.
- **One profile per real, staffed location** with permanent signage. No virtual offices, no PO
  boxes, no profile for a town where 305 SKY has no staffed hangar. **OPF:** a map pack listing
  there needs a real staffed location at Opa-locka. Until then, target OPF with an OPF page,
  organic search and AOG/mobile service content, not a fake profile.
- **Shared addresses are dangerous.** If a 305 SKY profile uses the same address as a competitor
  (e.g. Boca Aircraft Maintenance at 3300 Airport Rd, Boca Raton), Google tends to show only one
  of the two ("filtering"). Use a distinct suite/hangar number that appears on the lease and
  signage, and make sure the verification video shows 305 SKY's own space and signage.
- **No review gating, no paid or incentivized reviews, no reviews from staff or family, no
  review "farms".** Ask every real customer; never filter for happy ones first.
- **No fake competitor reviews, no editing competitors' profiles.** Report genuine guideline
  violations by competitors (keyword names, fake locations) through Google's redressal form.
- Change only one big thing on a profile at a time (name, address, category), and expect a
  re-verification when you do.

## Tracking

- Rank in the map pack depends on where the searcher stands, so track with a **geogrid**
  (Local Falcon, BrightLocal, Local Viking or similar): a 7×7 grid, about 0.5–1 mile spacing,
  centered on each airport, run every 2 weeks for each keyword.
- Weekly from GBP Performance: calls, website clicks, direction requests, search terms.
- Keep competitor counts (reviews, rating, categories, photos) in the scoreboard so the
  targets are concrete.
