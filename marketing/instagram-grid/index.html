The rules of the look

What keeps it luxe.

Palette

Three colors and nothing else. Gold is used like jewelry: a word, a line, a corner, never a whole background.

Black Gold Ivory Gold on ivory

Type

Cormorant Garamond for headlines, light weight, with one word in italic. Jost in spaced capitals for small labels. Never more than about 8 words on the cover.

Back in the sky.Return to service

Photo grading

Low key and warm. Bring the exposure down, lift the contrast, mute the color, and let light fall on the subject: a jet at dusk, a lit sign, hands on a panel. Shadows are where the mystery lives.

What we show, and don't

Real aircraft, real people, real hangars. Never stock jets, bright clip-art icons, busy collages, or neon. Tail numbers and owner details stay out of frame unless the owner has said yes in writing.

Logo

Small and quiet, top left or bottom corner. Light version on dark, standard on ivory. Never over an aircraft's livery, never as a watermark across the photo.

Reel covers

Instagram crops Reels to 3:4 in the grid. Every cover is designed to the grid first, so the profile stays composed even when most posts are video.

Recommendation: Option A, the checkerboard.

It gives you the dark, mysterious feel and the bright ivory feel on every screen. The rule is simple enough to follow without a designer: photos go dark, words go ivory, and posts alternate. Because Instagram has 3 columns, simple alternation makes a perfect checkerboard, and deleting or archiving a post only flips the pattern instead of breaking it.

Option C (Noir) is the most mysterious, but a whole feed of dark tiles can look heavy and hides the photography. Option B (bands) looks striking but breaks as soon as one post is out of order.
